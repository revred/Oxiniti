    How this is calculated

    In words — see plan/MODEL_SPEC.md for the equations.

    1. Pond water temperature

    Each district is matched to the nearest climate station. Monthly mean air temperature is converted to pond water temperature with a simple regression, since shallow tropical ponds track air temperature closely.

    2. Thermal suitability

    Each species has a tolerable range and a narrower optimum range. A month scores 1 when the water is within the optimum band, sliding down to 0 at the tolerable edges and beyond. The crop's average score, relative to the same species stocked at its default month in Tiruchirappalli, scales the harvest size up or down.

    3. Night-time oxygen budget

    For each crop month, the pond's dissolved oxygen at dusk is reduced overnight by fish respiration, plankton respiration and sediment demand, offset by any existing aeration and a small diffusion term. The result is the dawn dissolved oxygen the fish actually experience.

    4. Oxygen stress and relief

    A stress index compares dawn oxygen to the species' growth threshold and lethal limit. The OXY-Nano unit adds oxygen by mass balance from its published litres-per-minute rating, and the resulting relief — averaged over the crop — drives the uplift.

    5. Uplift, capped

    Relief is converted to extra growth and extra survival, calibrated so a fully oxygen-limited pond reaches the internal model's ceiling of +18 % extra saleable kilograms — never more. A partially-limited pond gets proportionally less.

    6. Economics and payback

    Extra harvest at your farm-gate price, less the extra feed and electricity it costs to produce, gives the extra profit per crop and per year. Payback divides the unit's capital cost (less any subsidy) by the extra annual profit; it stays blocked until you enter a real unit price.

    7. Sizing the units

    The number of units needed is set by the worst crop month's oxygen deficit, so the unit count reflects your pond's night-time chemistry rather than a flat per-acre rule.

    Known limitations

    • Monthly climate normals are used, not this year's actual weather.
    • Growth and mortality are modelled as linear through the crop; there is no disease, water-exchange or algal-crash model.
    • The uplift is calibrated to an internal conservative model, not measured on your pond.
    • Prices are the sourced range on the sourcing date; your own price overrides them.
